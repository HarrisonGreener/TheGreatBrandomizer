Pop Art colour palette and fonts

Pop Art came from the 1950s and 1960s, when artists borrowed from comic books, adverts and packaging. It's loud and graphic, with Ben-Day dots, thick black outlines, speech bubbles and flat primary colour. The palette is red, yellow, cyan and pink on black and white.

Where the Pop Art look works

Food and drink, toys, comics, streetwear, events and campaigns that want to grab attention fast.

Tips for using it

  • Thick black outlines and a halftone dot pattern sell the look straight away.
  • Comic-style headlines need space. Give them a speech bubble or a burst shape to sit in.

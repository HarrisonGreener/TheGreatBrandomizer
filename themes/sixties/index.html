Sixties colour palette and fonts

The Sixties theme covers the loud, joyful end of the decade: flower power, mod fashion and gig posters. Colours are saturated and warm, with tangerine, hot pink, sunshine yellow and turquoise sitting right next to each other. Type is chunky, rounded and bubbly, the kind of lettering that looks hand-drawn even when it isn't.

Contrast check

Of the 21 colour pairs in this palette, 2 pass WCAG AAA and 6 pass AA for body text. These are the strongest pairs for text:

  • Aa15.13:1 AAA#2A1638 on #FFF4DC
  • Aa11.41:1 AAA#2A1638 on #FFD21F
  • Aa6.51:1 AA#2A1638 on #1FB5AD
  • Aa6.42:1 AA#2A1638 on #B58CFF
  • Aa5.77:1 AA#2A1638 on #FF6A1A
  • Aa5.09:1 AA#2A1638 on #FF3FA0

AA needs 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text. AAA needs 7:1. How colour contrast works.

Where the Sixties look works

Fashion and vintage clothing, ice cream and sweets, festivals, beauty brands and anything aimed at fun rather than seriousness.

Tips for using it

  • Clashing is allowed here, but keep one calm colour, usually the cream or deep purple, as the base so the page can breathe.
  • Set the headline big and let the body font stay simple and readable underneath it.

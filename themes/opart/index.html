Op Art colour palette and fonts

Op Art, short for optical art, took off in the 1960s with artists such as Bridget Riley and Victor Vasarely. It uses precise stripes, checkerboards, circles and warped grids to make flat images seem to move, bulge or flicker. Most of it is black and white, with occasional bursts of saturated red, blue and yellow placed side by side to vibrate.

Where the Op Art look works

Fashion, music and club nights, galleries, eyewear, tech launches and any brand that wants to stop people scrolling.

Tips for using it

  • Build the effect from repetition. Thin stripes that bend or change width do more than any extra colour.
  • Keep text away from the busiest patterns. Put it in a solid block of black or white so it stays readable.

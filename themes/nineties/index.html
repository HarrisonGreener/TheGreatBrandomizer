Nineties colour palette and fonts

The Nineties theme borrows from Memphis-style patterns, Saturday morning TV and early desktop publishing: squiggles, confetti shapes and slightly off-kilter layouts. The colours are bold but a little dusty, with teal, purple, mustard and bubblegum pink. Type is comic, marker-drawn or chunky, with personality over polish.

Contrast check

Of the 21 colour pairs in this palette, 3 pass WCAG AAA and 6 pass AA for body text. These are the strongest pairs for text:

  • Aa15.74:1 AAA#1B1630 on #FBF3DC
  • Aa10.18:1 AAA#1B1630 on #9AD83A
  • Aa8.82:1 AAA#1B1630 on #E6B02E
  • Aa6.15:1 AA#1B1630 on #FF5FA2
  • Aa5.99:1 AA#7A3FB0 on #FBF3DC
  • Aa5.63:1 AA#1B1630 on #16A3A0
  • Aa3.87:1 AA large#7A3FB0 on #9AD83A
  • Aa3.35:1 AA large#7A3FB0 on #E6B02E

AA needs 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text. AAA needs 7:1. How colour contrast works.

Where the Nineties look works

Streetwear, snack and drinks brands, kids' products, event nights, podcasts and playful startups.

Tips for using it

  • Add one pattern made from simple shapes in your palette colours. It does a lot of the Nineties work for you.
  • Keep body text in a clean font even when the headline is goofy. Comic fonts are hard to read in paragraphs.

Victorian colour palette and fonts

Victorian graphic design grew out of the 19th-century print boom: woodtype posters, engraved labels and packaging that crammed every inch with ornament. Colour came from inks that were expensive and earthy, so palettes lean on oxblood, bottle green, tarnished gold and slate, printed on warm, slightly yellowed paper. Typefaces are high-contrast serifs and fat display faces with ball terminals and plenty of drama.

Where the Victorian look works

Craft gin and whisky labels, barbers, tattoo studios, antique shops, heritage food brands and anything that wants to feel established for a hundred years.

Tips for using it

  • Let the paper colour do the work. Use the light parchment shade as your background rather than pure white, and keep the dark ink for text.
  • Pair one theatrical display face with a quiet book serif. Two decorative faces fighting for attention is where Victorian designs tip into pastiche.

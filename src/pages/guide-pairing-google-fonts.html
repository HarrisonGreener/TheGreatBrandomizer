A good font pairing gives a brand its voice. Google Fonts has over 1,500 free families, which is wonderful and also overwhelming. These rules will help you pick two that work together, and the Brandomizer will suggest pairings that suit the style you're going for.

Start with two fonts, not five

Most brands need just two typefaces: a headline font with personality and a body font that's easy to read. A third is only worth adding for a clear job, such as a monospaced font for prices or code. Every extra font makes the brand harder to use consistently, and slows your website down.

Make them different, but not opposed

A pairing works when the two fonts have clearly different jobs. The classic move is contrast in style: a decorative or serif headline with a clean sans serif body, or the other way round. Two fonts that look nearly the same, such as two similar geometric sans serifs, read as a mistake rather than a choice.

At the same time, they should share something: a similar mood, era or proportion. A blackletter headline over a playful rounded body font sends mixed messages, unless that clash is the point.

Match the x-height

The x-height is the height of a lowercase "x". Fonts with similar x-heights look balanced next to each other, even at different sizes. If your body text looks tiny beside the headline at the same point size, look for a body font with a taller x-height.

Let the headline carry the style

The headline font does most of the stylistic work. That's why the Brandomizer spells out your brand name in the headline font on the top reels. The body font's job is to stay readable at 16 to 18 pixels for paragraphs of text. Fonts like Inter, Karla, DM Sans, Lora and Source Serif 4 are dependable body choices across many styles.

Use weights, not more fonts

If you need more variety, use more weights of the same families. A bold headline, a medium subheading and a regular body give plenty of hierarchy with just two fonts. Variable fonts on Google Fonts make this even easier, since one file covers every weight.

Check the licence

Almost every Google Font is released under the SIL Open Font License, which lets you use it for free in personal and commercial work, including logos, websites, print and products. You can't sell the font files themselves. It's always worth checking the licence on the font's Google Fonts page before you build a brand on it.

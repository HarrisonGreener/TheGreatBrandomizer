How to choose a brand colour palette

Most palette generators hand you five random colours and wish you luck. A brand palette needs more than that. It has to feel right for the business, work as text and backgrounds, and hold up on a website, a sign and a business card. Here's a simple way to get there.

1. Start with a feeling, not a colour

Before you open a colour picker, write down three words for how the brand should feel. "Warm, nostalgic, easy-going" points somewhere very different from "precise, fast, technical". Those words usually map onto a recognisable style. Warm and nostalgic is Seventies. Precise and technical is Tech or Swiss Style.

Starting from a style is the whole idea behind the Brandomizer's themes. Each theme has its own colour rules, so every spin stays inside a look that already works, instead of landing on colours that have nothing to do with each other.

2. Pick 5 to 7 colours, each with a job

Three colours is rarely enough for a real brand, and ten is too many to manage. Five to seven covers what you need:

  • A dark. Your main text colour. It doesn't have to be black. A deep brown, navy or plum often feels more considered.
  • A light. Your main background. Off-whites and creams are softer than pure white.
  • One or two main colours. These carry the brand: the logo, big shapes, headers.
  • An accent. Used sparingly, for buttons, links and anything you want people to notice.
  • One or two supporting tones. Muted shades for borders, cards and secondary areas.

Every Brandomizer palette is built to include at least one dark and one light, so you always have a readable text and background pair.

3. Use the 60-30-10 rule as a starting point

Interior designers use a simple balance: about 60 percent of a space in a dominant colour, 30 percent in a secondary one and 10 percent in an accent. It works for brands too. Usually your light background is the 60, your main brand colour the 30, and your accent the 10. Break the rule once you know why you're breaking it.

4. Check contrast before you fall in love

A palette that looks beautiful as swatches can be unreadable as text. The WCAG accessibility guidelines ask for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. The Brandomizer checks every pair in your palette when you cash out and labels them AAA, AA or large-text only. Our guide to colour contrast explains what those labels mean.

5. Test it in context

Put the palette on a fake website header, a business card and a social post before you commit. Colours shift a lot depending on how much of each you use. The brand preview and printed brand card in the Brandomizer are there for exactly this reason.

6. Write down the codes

Once you're happy, record each colour as a HEX code for the web, RGB for screens and, if you'll print, a CMYK or Pantone match from your printer. Give each colour a name and a role, such as "Ink" or "Accent", so anyone using the brand knows where it goes.

Try it

Pick a theme, pull the handle, and hold the colours you like while you re-spin the rest. When it feels right, cash out and copy the palette as CSS variables.
